Chapter 1 · The spark

1. The First Spark: How It All Began

Facebook circa 2010, where the question first took root
The early social media era made me wonder how the web really worked

In the early 2010s, social media was growing rapidly, and platforms like Facebook were becoming an integral part of everyday life. People were sharing photographs, writing posts, exchanging ideas, and connecting with friends in entirely new ways.

However, I began to notice a limitation. Social media posts often disappear into endless feeds, and meaningful information can quickly get buried beneath newer content. I started wondering whether there was a better way to preserve my ideas and share useful knowledge.

I realized that a personal website could offer something different—a lasting space where information could be organised, preserved, and accessed whenever needed. Instead of constantly pushing my ideas to people and hoping they would like my posts or follow my page, I wanted to create a place where people who genuinely needed particular information could find it, visit the website, and benefit from it.

That thought strengthened my belief in having a personal website: a place where knowledge remains accessible, ideas have room to grow, and people can discover useful information when they need it—not simply because it appears in their social media feed.

How does a website actually work behind the scenes?

Whenever I visited websites, especially government portals and informational sites, I wondered how their content got online and how people published text, images and documents for the world to see. That curiosity was the first step of a long road that eventually led to this blog.

Chapter 2 · Looking for answers

2. The First Curiosity: Understanding Websites

So I went looking for answers close to home. Many of my friends had studied technical subjects and worked in well-known IT companies such as Tata Consultancy Services and Infosys. Surely they would know how websites are built.

Surprisingly, when I asked how websites are created or how the backend works, most of them could not guide me. Working in a technology company, I learned, does not automatically mean working on web design.

My curiosity stayed strong anyway. I still wanted to understand how a website could show information to the entire world. The answer came from an unexpected place: a colleague.

Chapter 3 · A name of my own (2013)

3. Discovering Domains (2013) and Choosing a Name

Choosing a domain name, the first step to owning a piece of the web
saratoffice.in, bought from BigRock on 23 August 2013 for 10 years

In 2013, a colleague mentioned that he owned a blog website. I immediately asked whether he could show me how it worked from the backend. He explained that a web designer looked after the site and that he had only bought the domain name. That was the first time I learned:

Every website starts with a domain name.

The very next day I searched Google on how to buy one. After comparing options, I bought my domain from BigRock on 23 August 2013, for a 10-year term because the long-term plan was more economical.

As for the name, I wanted a personal space that reflected my professional interests, curiosity and love of learning: administration, office productivity, technology and personal growth, all in one place. So I chose:

saratoffice.in

Excited, I typed the address into my browser, expecting to see my website. Instead I got an error page.

Chapter 4 · The missing piece

4. Learning About Web Hosting

For many days I kept refreshing, assuming the site would switch on by itself. Finally I contacted BigRock's customer support. They confirmed the domain purchase was fine, and then the executive asked a question that changed everything:

"Where have you purchased the hosting?"

I had never heard the term. She explained that a domain name alone cannot run a website: you also need web hosting to store the files and make them reachable on the internet.

So the research began again. After months of comparing providers and plans, I bought shared hosting from BigRock, economical and compatible with my domain. Surely now the site would appear. It did not.

Chapter 5 · Another missing piece

5. The Challenge of Website Design

Designing a website takes far more than a domain and hosting
Code, layout and content: three pillars every site needs before going live

When I contacted technical support again, they explained one more important fact:

A website also needs to be designed.

Without design and content, my domain and hosting would stay empty. Support suggested hiring a web designer, but I was hesitant to share login details with someone I did not know. So I kept searching for guidance, and a few years later that search took me to a web development office in Bhubaneswar.

Chapter 6 · A dose of honesty (2016)

6. Advice From Web Developers (2016)

At a web development office in Bhubaneswar: honest advice that made me pause
Many developers pointed to simpler alternatives for my needs

In 2016, a friend and I visited Ratna Technology in Bhubaneswar. When I explained my idea, the developer gave surprisingly honest advice:

  • Photos and text could easily be posted on Facebook
  • Important websites could be saved with browser bookmarks
  • PDF files could be shared through Google Drive
He added that if I insisted, they could design the website, but I should first decide whether it was really necessary.

His honesty made me pause the project for a while. But the curiosity that started it all never faded, and soon enough it found a way forward.

Chapter 7 · It's alive

7. Finally Seeing the Website Live

After several attempts with different developers, one designer finally helped activate my domain. The day I typed saratoffice.in and watched it open was a moment of genuine satisfaction. It proved that the domain and hosting were working.

The first version was only a basic static page, but it was a real milestone. The domain was alive. What I still lacked was an easy way to manage and grow it myself.

Chapter 8 · Taking control (2016)

8. Discovering WordPress

The WordPress dashboard: publish articles, manage pages and upload media without code
On 12 October 2016, saratoffice.in was officially rebuilt on WordPress

Later, while helping a friend who needed a website for his business, I learned about WordPress. The developer explained that it lets a website owner:

  • Upload photos and videos directly from a browser
  • Publish articles and manage pages without any coding
  • Update content easily from a login dashboard
  • Manage documents and PDFs on your own domain
In many ways it feels similar to posting on social media, but on a space that belongs entirely to you.

That was the answer to the question that started everything. I began following WordPress tutorials on YouTube and gradually learned to manage a site on my own. That exploration shaped saratoffice.in into the platform it is today, officially rebuilt on 12 October 2016.

Chapter 9 · Looking back

9. Conclusion: Thank You for Visiting

The story of saratoffice.in is not simply about creating a website. It is a journey driven by:

🔍 Curiosity 💪 Persistence 📚 Continuous Learning

What began as a simple question about how content appears online grew, one step at a time, into a personal platform for organising knowledge, sharing resources and learning about web technology.

Sometimes, curiosity alone is enough to start a meaningful journey.

This is my digital space for knowledge, technology, public service and personal growth. Thank you for being a part of this journey, and this is just the beginning.
